    What it does not do

    Only byte-identical rows count as copies. Two rows describing the same thing with a different id are two rows here. That is a deliberate floor: near-duplicate matching needs a similarity threshold, and a threshold is a judgement call this tool refuses to make for you. In practice it means the count fires rarely — most providers attach an id or a timestamp to every record.

    No entity resolution. “Siemens AG” and “Siemens Aktiengesellschaft” are two entities here.

    No column roles are inferred. If you do not say which column is the date, the time span reads not determined and stays digit-free.

    No confidence score. There is no single number that says how good your data is. Five counts, each answerable, each traceable to rows you can open.

    No model. Nothing here is inferred, summarised or classified by a language model. That is why it costs nothing to run and why the same file always gives the same answer.

    No measurement of you. No cookie, no analytics, no request. Which also means nobody here knows how many people use this — that is the price of the promise, and it is paid on purpose.
